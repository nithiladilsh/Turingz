Why this is hard

The Problem MOTIVATION

Two families of PDE solvers exist, and each has a fundamental weakness the other does not. Neither alone gives fast and trustworthy long-horizon prediction.

Classical numerical solvers

Finite-difference, spectral, Cole-Hopf. Accurate & trustworthy, but computationally expensive for long-time simulation — they must take many tiny time steps.

Machine-learning solvers

PINN, FNO, DeepONet. Extremely fast after training, but only reliable inside the training window. Past it, error grows and they fail silently — plausible-looking but wrong.

The research gap. At deployment there is no ground truth to tell us when the ML model has failed. The hybrid must decide, on its own, when to trust ML, how to correct it with numerics, and how much numerical effort to spend. Those three decisions are exactly the three member modules.

The Extrapolation Gap REAL DATA

This is the failure the whole hybrid exists to fix. Watch a trained ML solver stay accurate up to t = 1, then diverge — producing a smooth, believable, wrong answer with no warning.

Worse than doing nothing. In the extrapolation window DeepONet's error (≈70%) is worse than a naive persistence baseline (53%) that simply freezes the solution at t=1. The operator has not learned the forward-time dynamics at all — clean, strong evidence of extrapolation failure.
The solution — one pipeline, three contributions

The Hybrid Engine SYSTEM

The fast ML model runs while it can be trusted; numerical computation is injected exactly when it is about to go wrong. Three independent modules make the three decisions, talking only through small fixed data contracts.
